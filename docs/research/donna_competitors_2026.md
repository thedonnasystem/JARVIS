## What the market tells us

1. **Two price bands, with a gap in the middle.** Self-serve tools cost $29 to $349 a month, but the owner builds and maintains them. Done-for-you vertical platforms (Sameday, Podium plus AI, Hatch, Avoca, Dialzara Done-for-you) start around $450 to $1,500+ a month and usually hide prices behind a demo. Donna sits in that middle as a done-for-you service with published prices.
2. **Nobody runs a founding offer.** None of the leaders publish one. A locked-in founding price is an open lane.
3. **Setup fees are rare.** Smith.ai, Ruby, Dialzara, Rosie and My AI Front Desk advertise no setup fee. A setup fee is an objection we have to answer.
4. **Booking sits in the middle tier.** Rosie and Sameday put calendar or system booking at the middle tier; Smith.ai charges $1.50 per booking. That is why Capture now includes booking.
5. **The biggest complaints:**
   - long contracts (Podium's 12-month auto-renew);
   - surprise overage and auto-upgrades (Smith.ai, Rosie);
   - owners left to tune the AI themselves;
   - AI that creates friction or confuses older callers (Jobber community thread).
6. **Risk reversal is weak across the board.**
   - Only Smith.ai publishes a money-back guarantee, and only on its human plans.
   - The others offer a 7-day trial at most.
   - The vertical leaders offer nothing published.
7. **Proof is case-study ROI.** Every leader sells on booked jobs and revenue, not on features. Donna has no clients yet, so it cannot claim results. Its proof must be the free missed-call audit: show each prospect their own missed calls.

## Why the discount is done this way (legal)

Canada's Competition Act (s.74.01(2) and (3)) and the Competition Bureau's November 2025 warning say a "regular" or "was" price must pass one of two tests:

- **Volume test:** more than 50% of sales were made at that price.
- **Time test:** the product was offered at that price in good faith for a substantial period.

The US FTC Guides Against Deceptive Pricing (16 CFR 233.1) say the same.

Donna has never charged the standard prices. So the site:

- **never** strikes through a price;
- **never** calls the standard price "regular" or "was";
- shows it only as the **upcoming** standard price, with "Save $X / month" measured against that upcoming price.

That stays truthful only if:

1. new clients actually pay the standard price once the 10 spots are filled;
2. the founding price is actually honoured for as long as the client stays;
3. dated records are kept: date each founding spot was sold, and date the standard price started.

If spots stay open indefinitely, the "upcoming price" becomes a fake reference price. [OWNER: confirm with counsel before launch]
